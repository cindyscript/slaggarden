Meaning of Labyrinth, according to Merriam-Webster:

- a place constructed or full of intricate passageways and dead ends. 
- Something extremely complex, or torturous. 


When I think of Labyrinths I think of:
- Dreams
- The pressure to make decisions
- Forbidden knowledge
- Anxiety
- Control
- A journey
- Navigating through a sea of people at an event


The origins {paraphrased, from my understanding}:
King Minos commissions Daedalus to construct a maze of confusing pathways, the labyrinth. The labyrinth keeps the minotaur imprisoned in the center. The minotaur was the result of a relationship between Queen Pasiphaë of Crete and the Cretan Bull, a white bull sent by Poseidon for King Minas to sacrifice. According to the myth, Pasiphaë has an unnatural desire for this bull due to being cursed by Poseidon, and thus resulted in the creation of Minotaur.

Minos's son was killed in battle so to appease Crete they demanded a tribute of 7 boys and 7 girls (or 9 in some cases) to be fed to the Minotaur.

Theseus was one of the men who was selected, and Minos's daughter Ariadne gives him a thread so that he can track his way in and out of the labyrinth. Theseus successfully slays the Minotaur.

- A need for control, a way to describe human nature
- War and conflict

- Consequences

- Making connections to things that humans cannot fathom or explain
### How do we make sense of a labyrinth concerning our daily lives and identity, the way we navigate?

A labyrinth for me is releasing. We have this fear and anxiety and we always try to go from point A to point B in a straight line but that is not always the case. We have to trust in ourselves to navigate the labyrinth of life. 

A labyrinth for me also is a neverending puzzle. The way that I present myself is wildly different from person to person. I keep myself in the center because I know that everyone can come in and leave, but I choose not to. I am in my own maze, my own labyrinth. I put these ever shifting walls around myself depending on the situation. I am never "truly" myself. I am quite content being at the center of this labyrinth but I know it has consequences. This leads to me isolating myself on purpose. I guess I do this as a form of self-preservation. 

**Analysis** - House of Asterion
*Jorge Luis Borges's House of Asterion* is a short story written from the perspective of Minotaur (Asterion). Asterion contradicts himself with his predicament. He can easily leave if he wanted to, and he does, only for the people to react in horror, but Asterion views that as they were relieved and happy to see him. He views himself as misunderstood. I would like to think that he is because of the way that he was born. We begin to sympathize and see ourselves in Asterion. Maybe Asterion has a form of arrogance about him as well 

To keep himself occupied he gives himself a tour of his labyrinth which is described as infinite. He projects himself as though he is giving another Asterion a tour as a remedy for his loneliness. 9 men will come in with the task to kill him, however, I do not think that Asterion thinks that they will kill him as he is happy to see them. But his brief moment of experiencing humanity is a form of violence, and he still kills them anyway only for one of the men to talk about the prophecy - the "redeemer" of Asterion. 

We forget that the narrator of this story is the bull-man-beast when the ending reveals that Theseus kills him with ease. He had long awaited for his redeemer and it finally came, only for his redeemer to say that he didn't put up a fight. Now we remember that Asterion is a bull-man-beast and not a human. The story written in the perspective of Asterion humanized him and turned Theseus into the monster.
