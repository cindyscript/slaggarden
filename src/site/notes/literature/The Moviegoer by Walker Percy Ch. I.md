The Moviegoer was published in 1961 by American author Walker Percy. 

Genres: philosophical, southern literature, existential 

## Chapter I:

The story starts off with the main character, John Bickerson "Binx" Bolling, living through the motions of his routine. He receives an invitation to lunch from his aunt, Emily. He seemed a bit reluctant at first, but knew it had to be a serious invitation and remembers how his brother had passed away from pneumonia as his Aunt Emily was there with him at the hospital. He was told to be strong. 

He talks about his girlfriends, who seemed displeased with him because he is daydreamy and in his own thoughts. His girlfriend Linda in particular is not pleased with him because he doesn't have a car, as he prefers to ride buses. Anyway, their relationship didn't last. He has a habit of dating his secretaries from work. Though I have a feeling that he calls them his "secretaries" in a demeaning way. Binx is a film buff and tends to go to the movies as much as possible. The movie he takes Linda to is about a man who lost his memory entirely and has to navigate a city and his life over again. I suppose this is how Binx felt, with his brother passing away suddenly at a young age. He talks a lot about his secretaries/girlfriends here and how eventually they grow apart and break up. 

Binx lives in Gentilly, Louisiana and works as a stockbroker. One morning he begins to think about "The Search". He had a night terror of his experience in the Korean War where he was shot, but "The Search" first came to him when he lay wounded. He promised himself that if he got up from the ground that he would resume "The Search". He gets ready to visit his aunt, looking at the everyday objects he carries (wallet, keys, notebook, pencil, slide rule) and begins to feel that he's seen these objects for the first time.

When I read that passage I felt that he was describing dissociation. The way that he described looking at them and seeing them for the first time on the dresser felt like tunnel vision. he thinks about "The Search" as he travels to his Aunts house. He goes a specific route to catch a film director William Holden in town as he was shooting scenes for a movie. However, it read to me that binx often planned out his specific routes, not sticking to one direction, because he wanders aimlessly or as a trauma response. He sees a pretty girl on the bus and remarks that if it were a movie he would have the opportunity to meet her. However, he goes back to thinking about "The Search" again. 

Binx isn't too sure about what "The Search" entails. He talks about how movies follow a certain formula: The main character is a castaway, the main character finds love, the main character is winning and life and successful, but eventually the main character sinks into everydayness. In Binx's mind, this is a form of despair for him, because then the story becomes mundane.
